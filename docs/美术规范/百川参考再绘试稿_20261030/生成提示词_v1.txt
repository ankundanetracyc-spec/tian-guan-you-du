Use case: stylized-concept, original game world-map background.
Create ONE continuous square terrain painting by freely redrawing reference 1 into an original Chinese ink-and-light-mineral-color CARTOGRAPHIC GAME MAP. This is a style and scale draft for an eventual seamless 3 by 3 world map. Do not draw a grid or nine separate panels.

REFERENCE ROLES:
1. The small cream-paper coastal map is the primary composition and overall restraint reference: vast connected plains, detached mountain regions, long eastern shore, delicate rivers, no tree clutter. Repaint it anew rather than enlarging its pixels. Omit its frame and watermark.
2. The brown Phantom Blade Zero screenshot is ONLY a material reference: ink absorbed into fine fibrous paper, softly broken brush edges, restrained mineral washes, hand-drawn contours. Do NOT copy its cliffs, camps, vegetation, buildings, brown darkness, UI, or close-up composition.
3. The colorful Mi Chang Sheng screenshot is ONLY a reference for geographical mountain depth: repeated overlapping silhouettes become lighter and simpler behind the nearer ridges. Do NOT copy its bright colors, UI, labels, icons or roads.

The most important requirement is HUGE TRAVERSABLE LAND AREA, seen from a very high oblique cartographic viewpoint, rather than a landscape viewed from the ground. Land dominates the square. Distribute a handful of detached mountain regions across northwestern, northeastern, western-middle and southwestern/south-central land, with a few modest foothill regions elsewhere; do not encircle the map with a mountain wall. Each mountain group occupies a SMALL footprint relative to the world, with gaps between groups much broader than their ridges. At least half the land reads as open, connected, gently washed plains and wide valleys. Give the regions distinct silhouettes and locally layered ridgelines: darkish fine charcoal-grey contours in front, medium grey sage-green silhouettes behind, very faint warm-grey farther ridges. All are painted ON a map surface, not photoreal terrain. Compact mountains can still have recognizable crags and rock folds. Avoid towering monumental peaks, dense spiky mountain carpets, and large foreground mountains.

A winding eastern coastline extends continuously from top to bottom, the muted grey-blue sea occupying only a narrow right-hand strip, around one eighth of the image. Coastline has a few natural bays, small capes and modest rocky islets, with restrained fine ink shorelines. Two thin, naturally winding rivers ENTER FROM OUTSIDE the LEFT EDGE, one near upper-middle, one near lower-left, traverse the broad plains and converge inland toward an estuary on the east coast. Rivers are delicate desaturated grey-blue watercolor with hairline banks, gently changing width, never bright cyan ribbons.

Material and color: pale warm ivory paper, delicate visible fine fibers at close inspection; hand-drawn controlled ink contours, lightly granulating low-saturation grey-green, faded ochre and pale slate-blue mineral pigment. More deliberate readable brush character than a blurry faded wash, but restrained contrast. Let the paper breathe. Plains contain only faint spacious changes in wash for geographical depth, NOT repetitive scratch marks or dirty mottling. No vignette, no dark yellow antique stain, no realistic sunlight or cast shadows, no 3D render, no horizon or sky, no photorealism.

Background layer ONLY. Absolutely NO TREES, forests, shrubs, grass tufts, bare trees, buildings, gates, pagodas, settlements, human figures, boats, bridges, stairs, mountain trails, roads, dotted routes, markers, wooden posts, text, calligraphy, symbols, grid, frames, borders, UI, logos or watermarks. Architecture and transit objects will be separate small front-facing interactive textures later, so leave this painting pure terrain. A coherent spacious playable map, not an ornate landscape illustration.
